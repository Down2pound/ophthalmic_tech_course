# Ten-Module Context Registry

These module records define the instructional boundaries for the existing
OptiTech Academy curriculum. They are starting contexts, not substitutes for
clinical source material or practice review.

## 1. Ophthalmic Foundations & Patient Communication

- **Learner starting point:** No assumed ophthalmology experience
- **Purpose:** Establish basic ocular terminology, the technician's role,
  patient-centered communication, privacy, safety, and escalation habits
- **Core outcomes:** Use foundational terms appropriately; prepare and identify
  patients according to practice policy; communicate respectfully; protect
  privacy; recognize role boundaries
- **Hands-on validation:** Patient identification, room preparation,
  communication, hand hygiene, and escalation workflow
- **Do not imply:** Completion authorizes independent clinical judgment

## 2. Refraction & Lensometry

- **Prerequisite:** Module 1
- **Purpose:** Introduce refractive terminology, optical principles, and the
  technician's supervised role in refraction and lensometry
- **Core outcomes:** Recognize sphere, cylinder, axis, prism, and add notation;
  explain the purpose of refraction and lensometry; record results accurately;
  identify inconsistent or questionable measurements
- **Hands-on validation:** Instrument setup, lens neutralization or measurement,
  transcription, cleaning, and repeatability
- **Escalate:** Unreliable measurements, unexpected changes, patient difficulty,
  equipment errors, or results outside practice-defined expectations
- **Do not imply:** The learner may independently prescribe or finalize an
  eyeglass prescription

## 3. Tonometry & Intraocular Pressure Measurement

- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1–2
- **Purpose:** Explain intraocular pressure measurement, common tonometry
  methods, patient preparation, infection control, and factors affecting
  reliability
- **Core outcomes:** Describe why pressure is measured; prepare the patient;
  follow device-specific instructions; document method, eye, time, and result;
  recognize when a measurement should be repeated or escalated
- **Hands-on validation:** Approved device operation, disinfection, positioning,
  documentation, and response to difficult measurements
- **Do not invent:** Universal emergency thresholds or device settings
- **Do not imply:** A pressure measurement alone establishes a diagnosis

## 4. Slit Lamp Examination & Anterior Segment Imaging

- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1–3
- **Purpose:** Introduce slit-lamp components, safe positioning, illumination,
  image acquisition, documentation, and equipment care
- **Core outcomes:** Identify major controls; prepare the patient and instrument;
  acquire images according to practice protocol; describe observable findings
  without diagnosing; recognize safety or equipment concerns
- **Hands-on validation:** Positioning, focus, illumination, cleaning, image
  labeling, and shutdown
- **Do not imply:** Online training establishes independent examination or
  diagnostic competency

## 5. Retinal Imaging & OCT Interpretation

- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1–4
- **Purpose:** Teach safe image acquisition, image-quality recognition, basic
  retinal and optical coherence tomography terminology, and appropriate
  escalation
- **Core outcomes:** Prepare the patient; select the assigned protocol; recognize
  common artifacts or poor-quality scans; label and save images correctly;
  distinguish technical observations from provider interpretation
- **Hands-on validation:** Device-specific acquisition, alignment, focus,
  laterality, scan selection, image quality, and data handling
- **Do not imply:** Technicians diagnose retinal disease or independently
  interpret OCT for treatment decisions

## 6. Visual Field Testing & Interpretation

- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1–5
- **Purpose:** Explain test purpose, patient instruction, reliability factors,
  artifact recognition, documentation, and escalation
- **Core outcomes:** Prepare and coach the patient; follow the assigned testing
  strategy; monitor fixation and reliability; recognize when results may be
  unreliable; document deviations
- **Hands-on validation:** Lens placement, occlusion, positioning, patient
  instruction, monitoring, saving, and cleaning
- **Do not imply:** Technicians independently diagnose field loss or alter the
  provider's testing strategy

## 7. Advanced Imaging & Specialized Procedures

- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1–6
- **Purpose:** Provide a framework for learning practice-approved specialized
  tests without treating all devices or procedures as interchangeable
- **Core outcomes:** Confirm the order and protocol; prepare the patient and
  equipment; follow manufacturer and practice instructions; document completion
  and deviations; escalate contraindications or uncertainty
- **Hands-on validation:** Separate supervisor validation for each device and
  procedure
- **Do not invent:** Device settings, contraindications, medication directions,
  or universal protocols

## 8. Patient Communication & Soft Skills

- **Prerequisite:** Module 1; informed by Modules 2–7
- **Purpose:** Strengthen empathy, plain-language explanations, de-escalation,
  accessibility, teamwork, and professional boundaries
- **Core outcomes:** Set expectations without promising outcomes; respond
  professionally to frustration; adapt communication appropriately; use
  teach-back; document and escalate concerns
- **Hands-on validation:** Role-play difficult encounters and receive manager
  feedback
- **Do not imply:** Staff should disclose confidential employee information or
  make clinical, financial, or scheduling promises beyond their authority

## 9. Clinical Documentation & EHR Proficiency

- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1–8
- **Purpose:** Teach accurate, timely, objective documentation and safe use of
  practice systems
- **Core outcomes:** Document what was observed, performed, reported, and
  escalated; distinguish patient statements from staff observations; correct
  errors according to policy; protect credentials and patient information
- **Hands-on validation:** Practice-system workflow using approved training data
  or de-identified scenarios
- **Do not include:** Real credentials, secrets, or patient identifiers
- **Do not imply:** Documentation may be altered to support a preferred billing
  level or clinical conclusion

## 10. Professional Development & Career Pathways

- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1–9
- **Purpose:** Consolidate professional expectations, continuing learning,
  competency development, feedback, and career planning
- **Core outcomes:** Create a development plan; distinguish course completion
  from certification; seek supervised practice; use feedback constructively;
  identify reliable continuing-education resources
- **Assessment:** Supports the 30-question final assessment and course completion
- **Do not imply:** The OptiTech certificate is licensure, JCAHPO certification,
  or proof of independent clinical competency
