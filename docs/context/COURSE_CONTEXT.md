# OptiTech Academy Course Context

This document is the authoritative context for creating, reviewing, or revising
OptiTech Academy educational content. It applies to the public academy and the
Spindel Eye Associates employee onboarding path unless a practice-specific
context explicitly narrows a policy.

## Program identity

- **Program:** OptiTech Academy — Ophthalmic Technician Foundations
- **Promise:** Better Training. Better Techs. Better Patient Care.
- **Format:** Ten-module online onboarding and competency-development course
- **Primary learner:** A new ophthalmic technician with little or no previous
  ophthalmology experience
- **Secondary learner:** An existing technician completing standardized
  education, remediation, or cross-training
- **Practice audience:** Independent ophthalmology and optometry practices,
  including the private Spindel Eye Associates onboarding path

## Program goal

Prepare technicians to support safe, efficient, compassionate patient care
while recognizing the limits of their role. Online completion demonstrates
course completion; it does not establish licensure, JCAHPO certification, or
independent hands-on clinical competency.

## Instructional principles

1. Teach one clearly defined skill or concept at a time.
2. Explain why the topic matters before presenting steps or terminology.
3. Define unfamiliar terminology in plain language before using it.
4. Connect information to realistic technician duties and patient encounters.
5. Use short sections, de-identified scenarios, worked examples, and job aids.
6. Distinguish knowledge that can be learned online from skills requiring
   supervised observation or performance.
7. Reinforce patient communication, accurate documentation, infection control,
   escalation, and professional conduct throughout the course.
8. Match assessments to stated learning objectives. Avoid trivia and trick
   questions.

## Clinical and privacy boundaries

- Never include protected health information or identifiable patient data.
- Never diagnose a patient or authorize an independent clinical decision.
- Never direct a learner to exceed practice policy, applicable law,
  manufacturer instructions, or provider supervision.
- Clearly identify findings, symptoms, equipment problems, and patient
  responses that require stopping, repeating, or escalating.
- Do not invent medication directions, procedure settings, clinical thresholds,
  or practice-specific policies.
- Label information that requires medical, legal, regulatory, or
  practice-leadership review.
- Preserve approved clinical facts exactly when revising tone or structure.

## Standard lesson contract

Every complete lesson should contain:

1. Lesson title
2. Two to five observable learning objectives
3. Why the topic matters
4. Essential terminology
5. A clear explanation or step sequence
6. A realistic, de-identified workplace scenario
7. A patient communication example when applicable
8. Common mistakes and how to prevent them
9. Stop, repeat, or escalate guidance
10. A knowledge check with explanations
11. A supervisor-observed competency recommendation for hands-on skills
12. Sources or clinical-review notes

## Assessment contract

- End-of-module questions must test the module's stated objectives.
- The academy final assessment contains 30 questions.
- The passing requirement is 80%.
- Each question must have one best answer unless it is explicitly marked
  "select all that apply."
- Distractors must be plausible but unambiguously incorrect from the lesson.
- Every answer must include a concise teaching explanation.
- A passed quiz does not replace supervised competency validation.

## Voice and reading level

Use a professional, encouraging, practical voice. Write for a beginner without
being childish. Prefer plain language, short paragraphs, descriptive headings,
and direct instructions. Explain abbreviations on first use.

## Context priority

When instructions conflict, use this priority order:

1. Patient safety, privacy, law, and professional scope
2. Approved clinical facts and current manufacturer instructions
3. This academy-wide context
4. The applicable module context
5. Practice-specific policies
6. The requested format or style

Practice-specific policies may narrow a workflow but must not override safety,
privacy, law, or approved clinical facts.

## Required review before publication

Content must pass the quality checklist in
[`QUALITY_CHECKLIST.md`](QUALITY_CHECKLIST.md). Clinical material and
practice-specific procedures must receive review from an appropriately
qualified practice representative before publication.
